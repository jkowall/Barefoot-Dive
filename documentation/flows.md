# Flows

## Local plan flow

1. Plan remains one primary workspace with Setup and Review views. In Setup, the diver enters a validated OC or CCR profile, gases, cylinders (or gases only), GF/settings, RMV, and reserve policy. CCR Setup has low and high setpoints, switch-up and switch-down depths (defaults 0.7/1.3 bar and 6 m/20 ft), the bailout trigger, and "Use diluent as bailout (dil-out)", which reveals a required, initially blank field for diluent used before bailout. Open-circuit Setup has a "Bottom RMV until first stop" switch, on by default, that charges the climb to the first stop at the bottom RMV (see the calculation model's gas accounting phases); it is hidden for CCR and in Cave. The "Gas planning" choice (Cylinders or Gas only) sits with the reserve policy, is hidden in Cave, and in Gas only hides cylinder source, capacity, and pressures while keeping mix, max PPO₂, and switch depth; a fixed-pressure reserve then shows an inline error with a "Use thirds" action and is never substituted silently. Switching to Gas only copies each Tank Bank-sourced gas's mix, name, and max PPO₂ into the draft, and the Tank Bank selection returns when switching back. Imperial switch-up and switch-down entries within half a foot of the 3 m stop grid snap to it (20 ft is 6 m), and a switch-down shallower than the loop can hold the high setpoint is deepened with a warning.
2. The first calculation is explicit. The UI resolves selected Tank Bank records into explicit gas/cylinder snapshots and calls the pure planner.
3. The engine simulates descent, bottom exposure, switches/setpoint changes, ascent, and stops. It emits event segments, tissues, ceilings, diagnostics, gas ledger, reserve crossings, and safety status. For CCR, the selected at-depth trigger state is cloned exactly and used to build the independent OC bailout plan.
4. Review shows the summary, warnings, analyzer assumptions, profile, runtime, gas/reserve sufficiency, and qualified-diver boundaries only for the result whose input signature matches the current Setup values. The runtime table folds consecutive segments with the same phase, end depth, gas, and setpoint into one row (a 15-minute stop appears once with its total time) and shows the Runtime column in whole minutes; the profile graph and the profile data table keep every emitted segment at mm:ss precision. Gas ledger volumes and cylinder descriptions follow the cylinder-capacity preference. A plan on the first-stop rule carries an info diagnostic saying so, and an open-water OC plan on the original rule (switched off, or saved before 0.5.0) shows a note under its gas ledger. Recalculating a saved plan keeps the rule it was saved with. A gas-only ledger adds a "Minimum to carry" column and marks each gas "Not checked (gas only)"; a dil-out diluent row names the volume deducted before bailout; a loop-only CCR primary plan says no open-circuit gas is breathed. Deco and bailout gases carry an "Include in plan" switch; a switched-off gas and its cylinder are omitted from the resolved input so the diver can see the plan without that gas, and its entry is kept for later. Under the cave thirds or sixths reserve policy the ad hoc cylinder minimum field is locked to the policy fraction of the starting pressure (or shows a higher entered minimum, which governs). The profile uses elapsed runtime for its horizontal scale and labeled depth measures, with phase/decompression bands, a phase and travel-duration summary, switch and reserve markers, and one directly labeled endpoint ceiling checkpoint for the segment currently being inspected.
5. After the first successful calculation, a direct valid input edit starts a short debounced recalculation. During the pending interval, or when the edited inputs are invalid, the previous numeric result and Save action are suppressed; the UI shows an updating or invalid state instead.
6. The Setup draft and latest matching Review result survive navigation among Plan, Cave, Tools, Tank Bank, and Saved Plans for the current app session. This session continuity is not a saved plan or backup.
7. A Tank Bank source revision never silently replaces the cylinder snapshot already used by the calculated Plan. The UI surfaces the newer source and requires an explicit update; only then does Plan recalculate against the revised record. A selected Tank Bank cylinder that no longer loads is never replaced by the gas editor's ad hoc values either; see [Tank Bank source resolution](#tank-bank-source-resolution-in-plan-and-cave).
8. The diver may save a local immutable snapshot only while Review matches the current Setup inputs.
9. Mouse, touch, and keyboard input can scrub any profile timeline. The docked readout shows the active emitted segment's phase, gas, plan mode, and setpoint plus linearly interpolated planned depth. The timeline summarizes bottom, decompression, and applicable descent, ascent, penetration, exit, bailout, or switch durations using the same colors as its phase strip. Only the inspected segment's exact endpoint ceiling is marked and labeled; numbered switch or reserve details remain tied to emitted events. Scrubbing preserves vertical page scrolling on touch devices and never changes the calculation or saved snapshot.

## Cave flow

The Cave workspace has distinct Setup and Review views. In Setup, the diver defines explicit penetration/exit route legs and the cylinders accessible on each leg, then chooses lost-back-gas, lost-buddy, scooter-failure, stage-failure, or CCR-loop-failure scenarios. The first calculation is explicit. Review is available only when the result signature matches every current route, scenario, gas, source, and limit input. After that first result, direct valid edits trigger a short debounced recalculation; superseded numeric output and saving remain unavailable while updating or invalid. A Tank Bank source revision remains an explicit update, never a silent recalculation, and an unavailable Tank Bank source blocks the cave calculation exactly as it blocks Plan. The current Cave draft and matching Review result survive primary-workspace navigation for the app session.

Route legs record cylinder access and the drop or recovery stage by gas, not by cylinder id; `src/app/caveRoute.ts` maps each gas to its current cylinder only when it builds the cave input. A leg that was never edited has every plan cylinder accessible. Its first access edit records the gases then taking part, as listed on the leg. A gas that takes part only later (a deco or bailout gas switched back on, a travel gas turned on, a newly added gas, or the other breathing mode's gases) starts inaccessible on that leg and is flagged without blocking the calculation: the leg names it with a "Keep not carried" action, Setup lists it under "Leg cylinders to confirm", and Review and a saved snapshot carry the `ROUTE_GAS_ACCESS_UNSET` warning. The flag clears once the diver ticks or unticks that gas on the leg or keeps it not carried; keeping it not carried leaves the cave input, and so a current result, unchanged. A library recalculation reruns the stored cave input unchanged, so each new revision carries these warnings forward from the revision it recalculates. A gas switched off after the edit keeps its recorded access for when it returns. Changing a gas's cylinder source (choosing a Tank Bank cylinder or another one, restoring an archived one, or detaching to ad hoc values) therefore never changes a leg's access or stage: the checkbox and the stage choice move to the gas's new cylinder. A stage whose gas is removed, switched off, or belongs to the other breathing mode shows "Select cylinder", and the cave layer rejects the drop or recovery until a stage is chosen; a recorded stage returns if its gas takes part again. Access and stages are set per cylinder, so one Tank Bank cylinder selected for more than one gas has no single answer. The status is then "Cylinder shared" (unless a source is unavailable, which takes precedence): Setup names the gases under "Shared Tank Bank cylinders", that cylinder's checkbox and stage option are locked on every leg (the checkbox shows a mixed state when the gases were recorded differently), and nothing is calculated, recalculated, reviewed, or saved until each gas has its own cylinder or the extra gases are switched off or removed. Each remaining gas then has the access and stage it had before. The planner also rejects two gases from one record.

The cave layer resolves the breathing cylinder for every route segment, applies stage drop/recovery state, derives exact route events and both operational and planned-turn constraints, and passes them to the production event planner. Gas-derived maximum time/distance is solved by repeatedly recalculating scaled candidates, including changed tissues, decompression, and per-cylinder gas use; it is omitted when a safe/unsafe bracket cannot be established. CCR limits and reserve margin use the actual bailout contingency ledger. Review aggregates the base plan and all enabled scenario diagnostics into its overall status before saving; choosing one scenario changes the detail view but cannot hide an error in another enabled scenario. The nested base and scenario outputs keep their summary metrics, diagnostics, and profile graphs visible and fold only their runtime and gas-ledger tables behind disclosures. Cave input, result, and cave-layer diagnostics are retained in saved snapshots, and any stored error reopens under an explicit unsafe banner. A cave ceiling-safe route must be explicitly modeled; the route layer does not infer a safe overhead exit from an open-water result. Cave results are experimental decision support pending qualified cave-diver review, not a cave-procedure, safety, compatibility, or parity claim.

Primary, CCR bailout, cave-base, cave-scenario, and stored base-plan result views share the same emitted-data graph and scrub interaction. The readout reports the active segment's emitted state, a linearly interpolated planned depth, and endpoint checkpoint values from the calculated result. Continuous cylinder pressure, GF, tissue, PPO₂, and exposure state remain outside the graph contract.

## Tank Bank source resolution in Plan and Cave

1. Plan and Cave read every Tank Bank record, archived ones included, when the workspace opens and after any Tank Bank change. Only loaded, unarchived records are offered as cylinder sources. These reads never write.
2. `resolvePlanInput` in `src/app/planning.ts` resolves each gas that takes part in the calculation: the bottom gas, an enabled travel gas, and included deco gases, or the diluent and included bailout gases. A gas with no selected cylinder uses its ad hoc fields; a gas whose selected record loads uses a snapshot of that record. Tank Bank names a record's gas identifier after the gas (`Air` is `air`) and Duplicate copies it, so the gases of two different records can share one, for example a CCR air diluent and an air bailout. The first of them in plan order keeps the identifier and each later record's gas is renamed `air~2`, `air~3`, and so on, in the calculation input and any saved snapshot only; the Tank Bank records keep theirs. Among identical mixes from such records the planner then prefers the first listed, for example back gas over a pony of the same mix. Gases taken from one record keep one identifier, so that input is still rejected ([architecture](architecture.md#storage-and-ui)).
3. A selected record that cannot be used is an unavailable source, for one of four reasons: it is archived; it no longer exists (deleted); it is quarantined; or the Tank Bank could not be read ([read and quarantine flow](#tank-bank-read-and-quarantine-flow)). A quarantined record is matched by the id in its `STORAGE_RECORD_QUARANTINED` diagnostic and named by its stored name; one without a readable id reports as no longer existing. For any reason, the resolver returns no calculable input, only the unavailable sources and one `TANK_SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE` error diagnostic per gas. It never substitutes the gas editor's ad hoc fields.
4. While any source is unavailable, the workspace status is "Source unavailable". Calculate, Update, automatic recalculation, Review, and saving are unavailable, and no earlier result is shown. Setup lists every affected gas under "Tank Bank sources unavailable". Plan also keeps Gas only off, because an unavailable record has no mix to carry into a gas-only plan. Excluded deco or bailout gases, a travel gas that is off, gases of the other breathing mode, and gas-only plans take no Tank Bank source and are never blocked.
5. The affected gas editor states the reason and lists the ad hoc gas and cylinder values that detaching would calculate with: gas, O₂, He, capacity, working, starting, and minimum pressure (the reserve-policy value under cave thirds or sixths), and cylinder max PPO₂. The list comes from the same resolver path as the calculation. The cylinder-source control names the unavailable record instead of showing an unrelated option, and the editable ad hoc fields stay hidden until the diver acts.
6. The diver resolves it explicitly: choose another cylinder in the source control, restore the record in Tank Bank, or choose "Detach to ad hoc values" (or "Ad hoc plan cylinder"), which clears the selection and shows the listed values as editable fields. Tank Bank "Use" copies only the gas name and mix into a new Plan, and choosing a source copies nothing, so detached cylinder values can differ from the record and must be checked against the physical cylinder.
7. Once every source resolves, a workspace with no calculation offers Calculate. One calculated earlier shows "Source changed" and recalculates only after an explicit Update, even when the record and every input return unchanged: the unavailable episode marks that calculation as superseded for the rest of the session.
8. In Cave, every way of resolving a source (choosing another cylinder, restoring the record, or detaching) keeps each route leg's cylinder access and stage with the gas, because legs record them by gas ([Cave flow](#cave-flow)). While the source is unavailable, the leg lists the ad hoc cylinder a detach would use, with the gas's recorded access.

## Tools flow

The user chooses a task category and capability in the focused Tools workspace. Simple tools recalculate locally as their session inputs change and expose assumptions and warnings. Tank Bank selection copies gas/cylinder data into an ephemeral session snapshot; the user may visibly detach and continue with manual values. A result is not a plan mutation until the user chooses an exact Plan action and confirms its before/after preview. The allow-list is Best Mix to the OC bottom-gas name/O2/He, SAC / RMV to one selected RMV target, and OC Rock Bottom / Minimum Gas to OC rock-bottom assumptions.

Emergency Gas has a stricter flow. The user enters an explicit ascent/stop schedule and the RMV/team assumptions, optionally adding cylinder context for OC gas-pressure bounds; CCR Simplified Bailout requires one cylinder. The current input snapshot recalculates automatically after a short pause. During that pause the old result is not displayed and Plan application is disabled; invalid inputs produce current diagnostics instead of preserving an old answer. It is not a generated decompression or bailout plan. Any safety or compatibility interpretation still requires independent fixtures, analyzer-first verification, and qualified review.

## Tank Bank read and quarantine flow

1. Tank Bank, Plan, Cave, and Tools read the same stored Tank Bank. Reads never write.
2. If the stored container cannot be read (a storage read error, corrupt JSON, not a record list, an unsupported schema version, or a legacy bare array with an invalid record), the read fails whole. Tank Bank shows "Tank Bank could not be read" with the diagnostic instead of an empty bank and disables Add cylinder and Save cylinder; every write is refused, so the stored data stays as it was. Plan, Cave, and Tools offer no Tank Bank records. When local storage is missing entirely at startup, Tank Bank instead shows "Tank Bank unavailable".
3. Otherwise each record is validated on its own. Valid records load everywhere. An invalid record is quarantined: Tank Bank shows a "Quarantined cylinder records" notice naming its stored position, its name or id, and the failing fields, and the record is not listed in the Active or Archived view or offered to Plan, Cave, or Tools.
4. Creating, editing, duplicating, archiving, restoring, or deleting a valid cylinder writes the bank back with every quarantined record's exact stored text, in the same order relative to the other records. The app cannot edit or delete a quarantined record; the diver re-creates the cylinder to plan with it.
5. A Plan or Cave gas already sourced from a record that stops loading, because it is quarantined or the bank cannot be read, is held out of every calculation until the diver chooses another cylinder or detaches it; see [Tank Bank source resolution](#tank-bank-source-resolution-in-plan-and-cave).
6. Saved Plans do not quarantine. One invalid saved record makes the library unreadable and blocks saving new snapshots until the stored data is repaired or cleared.

## Local/offline and native flow

The PWA service worker serves the built assets after installation. Capacitor shells serve the same local bundle. Tank Bank and saved plans read/write browser/WebView `localStorage`. There is no account check, network calculation, sync, analytics, subscription, signing, store submission, or deployment flow.
