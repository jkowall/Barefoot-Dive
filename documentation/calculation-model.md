# Calculation model

## Implemented model

The pure engine uses 16-compartment ZH-L16C inert-gas kinetics for nitrogen and helium, with coefficient metadata identifying the DecoTengu/OSTC-derived set (`decotengu-0.14.1-coefficients-v1`). It tracks tissue state through explicit exposure events, computes GF-adjusted ceilings, rounds stops to the configured grid, and schedules deterministic ascent/stops. Inputs and outputs use branded metres, seconds, bar absolute, litres, and fractions.

Breathing events support OC bottom/travel/deco gases, CCR diluent/setpoint exposure, CCR setpoint changes, and bailout. The CCR bailout plan starts from the exact tissue/depth/runtime state at `bailoutTriggerSecondsAtDepth`. Gas accounting integrates ambient pressure over each segment and applies fixed, custom, thirds, sixths, or rock-bottom reserves, reporting insufficiency and reserve-crossing diagnostics.

### CCR setpoints (engine 0.2.0)

With `lowSetpointBar`, the loop is closed at the low setpoint from the surface, switches up to `setpointBar` at `setpointActivationDepthM`, and switches down when leaving `setpointDeactivationDepthM` on ascent (absent means the switch-up depth). The rule is "leave the depth": the high setpoint is held at every depth at or below the switch-down depth, including a stop there, and the switch happens immediately before the next ascent leg. A switch-down depth that falls between stops or shallower than the last stop splits that leg without adding a stop, in the no-stop path, the first-stop search, the stop loop, and the final leg. In low-setpoint mode a setpoint change is exposed on the lower of the two setpoints for the convention's switch duration (0 s, or 5 s under the Shearwater preset); legacy inputs keep the 0.1.0 exposure on the new setpoint. Loop inert pressure is `ambient − water vapor − setpoint`, split by the diluent's inert ratio; where the setpoint exceeds that, the loop is modeled as oxygen at ambient pressure. That clamp would credit off-gassing the loop cannot deliver, so the open-water planner never holds the high setpoint shallower than its achievable depth, `(setpoint + water vapor − surface pressure) × metres per bar` (3.6 m for 1.3 bar at sea level). A shallower switch-down depth, including 0, is deepened to that depth with a `CCR_SWITCH_DOWN_DEEPENED` warning; this also keeps the clamp out of the no-stop decision. Explicit event plans apply the same floor to the setpoint actually held when the ascent starts. Because the low setpoint can load inert gas on the rest of a leg that crosses the switch-down depth, the arrival re-check (see Arrival ceilings) moves the first stop one grid step deeper, or holds the current stop on the high setpoint, until the arrival clears. The low setpoint must be 0.5–1.6 bar, not above the high setpoint or the bottom PPO₂ limit, and achievable at the surface. A diluent hypoxic at the surface is accepted on the loop with a flush warning; a diluent whose own PPO₂ exceeds a setpoint is flagged because the model then carries more inert gas and less oxygen than the loop can hold.

Without `lowSetpointBar` the legacy convention applies: open-circuit diluent from the surface to the activation depth and again after arriving at it on ascent, with the diluent required to be breathable over that range. Legacy CCR breathing is unchanged from engine 0.1.0. Legacy CCR and non-hypoxic OC schedules are also unchanged, except where the engine 0.2.1 arrival re-check applies (see Arrival ceilings): mostly bailout plans, and open-circuit switches from a nitrogen gas to a helium-bearing one. `src/engine/legacy-golden.test.ts` guards seven fixture inputs against digests captured from 0.1.0, and the 0.2.1 re-check leaves all seven unchanged; it is a regression guard, not a proof over all inputs. Breathing air at a 6 m stop, the two slowest compartments settle at about 1.21 bar nitrogen, above their surfacing limits at GF high 0.70 (1.20 and 1.19 bar). Once they are loaded past those limits the stop cannot clear, so after long, deep dives on an air loop the legacy loop plan is rejected with `DECOMPRESSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (unchanged since 0.1.0). Inputs without the new fields can still gain `CCR_DILUENT_PPO2_ABOVE_SETPOINT` and `CCR_BAILOUT_TRIGGER_PPO2_HIGH` warnings, can be rejected by `CCR_BAILOUT_SURFACE_REQUIRED`, `CCR_BAILOUT_COVERAGE_GAP`, or `OC_GAS_HYPOXIC`, can be rerouted by the hypoxic waypoint or the stop move below, and are rejected when their bailout plan cannot finish decompression.

### Bailout, dil-out, and hypoxic legs

The effective bailout set is `bailoutGases`, plus the diluent when `diluentBailout` is true. Gas choice is richest oxygen, then least helium; for an identical mix a dedicated bailout is preferred over the diluent. Validation requires at least one bailout gas that is switch-eligible (breathable and no deeper than its switch depth) at the target depth and at the surface, and the union of every gas's eligible depth interval must cover the depths between (`CCR_BAILOUT_COVERAGE_GAP` names the first gap). A trigger gas above the bottom PPO₂ limit is flagged but not reselected. For dil-out the diluent needs its own cylinder and a diver-entered `diluentPreBailoutUseL`; the bailout ledger starts that cylinder at its full volume minus the entered use and any modeled open-circuit diluent breathing before the trigger, and keeps the reserve policy on the full volume. Loop make-up, ADV, flush, wing, and suit use are not modeled.

An open-circuit ascent leg whose end would put the breathed gas below the minimum PPO₂ is split at the deepest depth, no shallower than that gas's hypoxic floor, where a preferred gas is switch-eligible, and breathing is updated there. Legs that stay breathable are committed exactly as before. Every plan (primary, bailout, and cave event plans) is then scanned, and any open-circuit segment below the minimum PPO₂ at either end is a fatal `OC_GAS_HYPOXIC`. One tolerance (1e-8 bar) is shared by the scheduler, validation, and this guard.

A stop can fail to clear on its gas, for example a lean trimix at the last stop when the richer gas is eligible only shallower. When a schedule would reach the 48-hour limit, the ascent is recomputed once. At the stuck stop depth or shallower, a stop that cannot clear may move to the deepest shallower depth where a preferred open-circuit gas becomes switch-eligible, if that depth is inside the ceiling; deeper stops are scheduled exactly as in the first run. Decompression continues there, which can be shallower than the configured last stop, and the plan carries `OC_STOP_MOVED_FOR_GAS_SWITCH`. Schedules that already clear are never recomputed. A CCR plan whose bailout plan still reaches the 48-hour or iteration limit is rejected, with the failure attributed to the bailout plan.

### Arrival ceilings (engine 0.2.1)

The first-stop search targets the GF-low ceiling rounded deeper to the stop grid. When a gas-switch depth or CCR activation depth lies between the current depth and that target, the leg ends at the deepest such boundary instead. Each leg is committed with the Schreiner equation, and the ceiling is recomputed after it. Tissues can still take up inert gas during a leg. After a switch from a nitrogen-loaded gas or loop to a helium-bearing one, the fast compartments take up helium faster than they release nitrogen, because each ZH-L16C compartment's helium half-time is about 2.65 times shorter than its nitrogen half-time. Total inert tension can then rise while the diver ascends. A mid-leg switch down to the low setpoint has the same effect. Every mode therefore re-checks the arrival:

- **First-stop search:** a leg that arrives above the GF-low ceiling is discarded, then retried from the pre-leg state one grid step deeper. When no shallower grid depth clears, the current depth becomes the first stop.
- **Stop loop:** a leg is taken only if two checks pass: the tissue state permits the next depth before the leg, and the arrival still clears at that depth's gradient factor. Otherwise the stop is held for another time quantum. The stuck-stop move to a richer gas applies the same arrival check.

Before engine 0.2.1 only low-setpoint legs were re-checked. For example, take a legacy CCR bailout from 60 m after 10 minutes (air diluent, 1.3 bar, Tx12/60, then EAN50 from 21 m). It stopped first at 27 m, where it arrived under a 29.08 m ceiling, then held that stop under 27.76 m. In that leg the fastest compartment controls the ceiling: its helium rises from 0 to 2.37 bar while its nitrogen falls only from 4.95 to 3.29 bar. The bailout now stops first at 33 m, arriving under a 31.20 m ceiling; 30 m also fails on arrival.

**A deeper first stop can shorten the schedule.** The gradient-factor line is anchored at the first stop (Baker's definition), so a deeper first stop gives every shallower stop a higher gradient factor. The deeper anchor comes from a brief helium surge in the fast compartments. The shallow stops it relaxes are controlled by the slow compartments. In the 60 m example, time to surface falls from 46.7 to 46 minutes. With air at 40 m for 60 minutes, then Tx30/30 from 36 m and oxygen from 6 m, at GF 20/85, the effect is larger. Engine 0.2.0 arrived 0.007 m above the GF-low ceiling at 24 m. Engine 0.2.1 stops first at 27 m, and time to surface falls from 109.8 to 103.5 minutes. The fix therefore removes arrival breaches but is not uniformly more conservative. How to anchor the gradient-factor line after a deepened first stop is an open decision in the ROADMAP.

Any ascent that takes up inert gas during a leg can change. That includes open-circuit plans that switch from a nitrogen gas to a helium-bearing one, CCR bailouts, and event and cave ascents. Two exploratory comparisons against engine 0.2.0 were run: the implementer's (about 115,000 plans) and the scientific reviewer's (about 30,000). Their harnesses are not committed, so they indicate scale only and are not fixtures.

- **Implementer's comparison:** this grid mostly exercised helium bottom gases on open circuit, so its open-circuit and normal CCR schedules did not change. About 4% of its bailout plans changed, every one of which had previously ended a leg above its ceiling. First stops moved 3–18 m deeper and never shallower. Time to surface changed in both directions, with a median of about +2 minutes, extremes from 30 minutes shorter to 217 minutes longer, and roughly 40% of changed plans shorter.
- **Scientific reviewer's comparison:** this added GF 10/90, low-setpoint and nitrox loops, helium-bearing open-circuit deco gases, and event plans. It found open-circuit and event-plan changes and reductions of up to 55 minutes. About 35–55% of changed plans were shorter, depending on the GF pair.
- **Both:** no plan's ok or error status changed, and no plan's worst breach grew.

The re-check covers each arrival. The scheduler does not look ahead through a stop. After a switch from a nitrogen-rich loop (air or nitrox) to a lean, helium-heavy bailout gas, the ceiling can keep deepening for several minutes while the diver is stationary. Holding the stop cannot correct that. For example, take 30 minutes at 75 m on an air loop, bailing to Tx10/70 at GF 20/85. The ceiling reaches 61.49 m during the 60 m first stop, and is past the stop for 7 of its 16 minutes, at GF 0.230 against a GF low of 0.20. In the implementer's comparison, where the lowest GF low was 0.20, this happened only from 75–100 m, by up to 4.8 m. The reviewer's broader grid found it from 70 m and on nitrox loops. It also found 7.0 m at GF 10/90 (GF 0.197 reached against 0.10), and a 0.16 m breach after a bailout to Tx18/45 from 50 m. No diagnostic is emitted; the per-segment ceiling in the profile shows it.

### Gas accounting phases

The ledger integrates ambient pressure over each segment at one of two RMVs. These are the engine 0.1.0 rules, which still apply to every plan that does not opt in to the first-stop boundary below:

- **Open circuit:** descent and bottom segments use the bottom RMV (`rmv.bottomLpm`). Every segment that starts at or after the end of bottom time uses the deco RMV (`rmv.decoLpm`). That includes the ascent from the bottom to the first stop, and the whole ascent of a no-stop dive. The normal CCR ledger applies the same rule to its open-circuit diluent segments.
- **CCR bailout:** only stops use the bailout deco RMV (`rmv.bailoutDecoLpm`). Every travel leg uses the bailout RMV (`rmv.bailoutLpm`): the ascent to the first stop, the moves between stops, and the final ascent.
- **Volume split:** `bottomUsedL` and `decoUsedL` report the volume charged at each rate.
- **Rock-bottom reserve:** a separate calculation. It charges `teamSize × stressedRmvLpm` over every ascent, exit, bailout, and stop segment, plus every open-circuit segment after the end of bottom time, so it already covers the climb to the first stop at the stressed rate.
- **Tools:** Emergency Gas applies its one entered stressed RMV to every entered segment, with no bottom or deco phase.

**The climb to the first stop is charged at the deco RMV.** The ascent from the bottom is usually the deepest travel of the dive, and the diver is not yet resting at a stop. Charging it at the relaxed deco rate understates the expected use of the gas breathed there, usually the bottom gas. For one climb the gap is `(bottom RMV − deco RMV) × climb minutes × mean absolute pressure of the climb`. At the default 20 and 15 L/min, a 45 m Tx18/45 dive with its first stop at 24 m climbs for 2.3 minutes and is short 52 L of bottom gas, 2.2 bar on a 24 L twinset. At 60 m with a 30 m first stop the gap is 92 L; at 80 m with a 45 m first stop it is 141 L, 5.9 bar. In these examples that is 1.4–3.3% of bottom-gas use and under a minute of equivalent bottom time. The share is largest on a short no-stop dive, where the whole ascent is affected: 30 m for 8 minutes on air is short 42 L of 848 L, 4.9%. The gap grows with depth and with the difference between the two rates. It matters most under the thirds, sixths, fixed, and custom reserves, where expected use sets the margin directly; rock bottom already charges the climb at the stressed rate.

Planners differ here. MultiDeco's help assigns the bottom RMV to the descent, the bottom, and the climb to the first deco stop, and the deco RMV to the deco stops. Subsurface charges every point it generates after the diver-entered profile, the climb included, at the deco SAC. The Shearwater Petrel 3 onboard planner uses one RMV for the whole dive. The engine 0.1.0 rule matches Subsurface. [`reference-validation.md`](reference-validation.md) lists these sources; none of them is a parity claim.

**First-stop boundary (engine 0.3.0).** An open-water OC plan can set `decoRmvFrom: "first-stop"`. The ledger then charges the bottom RMV until the diver arrives at the first stop, or for the whole ascent on a no-stop dive, and the deco RMV from the first stop on, including the moves between stops. A gas switch made on arrival at the first-stop depth counts as part of the stop. This is the same assignment MultiDeco's help describes; no MultiDeco output comparison was made. Only the charged rate and the `bottomUsedL`/`decoUsedL` split move; segments, stops, and tissues are identical. Cylinder reserves are identical too: fixed, thirds, and sixths depend on the starting volume, a custom reserve is entered, and rock-bottom membership keeps the engine 0.1.0 phase, so a 5 s gas-switch segment during the climb still counts toward rock bottom. A reserve crossing can only move earlier. Gas-only planning derives its thirds and sixths reserves from expected use, so they rise by half and by twice the added volume, and the minimum to carry by 1.5 and 3 times it; custom and rock-bottom minimums rise by the added volume alone. A gas switched to during the climb is charged at the bottom RMV until the first stop too. Under the Shearwater preset, a 30 m air dive whose climb to a 12 m first stop crosses the 21 m EAN50 switch charges 16 L more air and 13 L more EAN50. A plan that uses the boundary carries the info diagnostic `DECO_RMV_FROM_FIRST_STOP`. Validation rejects any other value (`DECO_RMV_BOUNDARY_INVALID`) and the field on a CCR plan (`DECO_RMV_BOUNDARY_OC_ONLY`); the bailout ledger keeps charging only stops at the bailout deco RMV. Cave plans also reject it (`DECO_RMV_BOUNDARY_CAVE_UNSUPPORTED`) until cave turn limits have been reviewed with it. Without the field the engine 0.1.0 rule applies, so stored plans recalculate unchanged and the seven legacy digests are unaffected. From app 0.5.0 the Plan workspace sets the field for every new open-water OC plan, and its Setup switch Bottom RMV until first stop turns it off; the Cave workspace never sets it. Recalculating a saved plan keeps the rule it was saved with; to use the new rule for an older plan, enter it again in Setup. The boundary is a gas-accounting convention, not a measured breathing rate; no ascent-RMV study was reviewed.

### Gas-only planning

`gasOnly` plans (open water only) carry gases without cylinders; a per-gas `maximumPPO2` replaces the cylinder PPO₂ ceiling. The ledger reports used volume, the reserve, and `requiredVolumeL`, the minimum surface volume to carry, by inverting the cylinder rules: thirds keeps a third of the start, so the start is 1.5 × used; sixths keeps two thirds, so 3 × used; a custom reserve is added; rock bottom uses the same formula over that gas's ascent, deco, exit, and bailout segments. A fixed minimum-pressure reserve needs a cylinder size and is rejected. A gas whose reserve comes out as zero, such as a travel gas under rock bottom (which counts only ascent, deco, exit, and bailout use), is flagged `GAS_ONLY_RESERVE_ZERO`. A per-gas `maximumPPO2` is accepted only in gas-only plans (`GAS_MAXIMUM_PPO2_REQUIRES_GAS_ONLY`); cylinder plans take the limit from the cylinder. Capacity, pressures, unusable residual gas, per-cylinder minimums, and reserve crossings are not checked, and such entries are never reported as sufficient. Dil-out and cave plans require cylinders.

### Cave

The cave module supplies explicit penetration and exit events and scenario transformations to the same production event engine. Each route segment resolves a physically accessible, depth-eligible gas/cylinder; stage drops and recovery determine later accessibility. Gas-derived maximum time/distance uses a bounded search over proportionally scaled copies of the entered route. Every candidate is rerun through the tissue engine, ascent/decompression scheduler, and every actually used cylinder ledger; CCR candidates use the exact end-of-penetration bailout plan. A numeric maximum is emitted only after a safe lower bound and unsafe upper bound are both established. Otherwise the result carries `GAS_LIMIT_UNDETERMINED` and omits the number. The reported turn pressure and minimum-required pressure identify the same actual cylinder, while the result separately identifies the cylinder with the limiting reserve margin.

Thirds and sixths expose their operational turn thresholds separately from the minimum pressure required at the planned turn. The CCR cave-level ledger is the bailout contingency ledger; normal CCR oxygen metabolism and diluent/loop consumables remain explicitly unmodeled. The module does not invent a ceiling-safe overhead route. A cave route must explicitly model the route and be checked; cave behavior is experimental pending qualified cave-diver review. In low-setpoint CCR mode, descent and penetration legs switch up at the activation depth, exit legs switch down at the switch-down depth, and the final ascent stays on the loop, so no open-circuit surface gas is required for it. The switch-down depth must be at or below the depth where the high setpoint is achievable (`CAVE_CCR_SWITCH_DOWN_TOO_SHALLOW`), because route legs are explicit. CCR turn limits come from the bailout ledger alone and do not subtract loop breathing on the diluent cylinder. With dil-out, cave gas choice uses the planner's shared ordering (richest oxygen, then least helium, then a dedicated bailout before the diluent); other cave plans keep the 0.1.0 ordering. The dil-out diluent cylinder's operational turn pressure is never below the pressure its exit and reserve require, because its gauge falls during penetration and its exit is on open circuit at bailout RMV. The penetration-limit search scales the entered pre-bailout diluent use with each candidate route, never below the entered value.

## Presets and limitations

`barefoot-zhl16c-v1`, `multideco-zhlc-compatible-v1`, and `shearwater-petrel3-v103-compatible-v1` are scheduling/settings presets with explicit experimental status. Their labels do not claim MultiDeco, Shearwater, firmware, or schedule parity. The implementation does not include repetitive dives, planner CNS or OTU accumulation, IBCD validation, or arbitrary profile authoring as validated capabilities. Low-setpoint modeling changes PPO₂ exposure, but the planner reports no CNS or OTU total; CNS is available only in Tools. Every ascent leg re-checks its arrival ceiling (engine 0.2.1), but the scheduler does not look ahead through a stop. After a switch from a nitrogen-rich loop to a lean, helium-heavy bailout gas, the ceiling can deepen past a held stop for several minutes, without a diagnostic; exploratory comparisons found this by up to 7.0 m, from about 70 m (see Arrival ceilings). A deepened first stop also anchors the gradient-factor line deeper, so the re-check can shorten a schedule as well as lengthen it.

The 11 calculator functions are production code paths, but their existence and unit tests do not establish operational safety. CNS uses the documented NOAA single-exposure table only from PPO2 0.6 through 1.6 bar. Barefoot Dive linearly interpolates between the listed rows as an explicit versioned implementation policy; NOAA is not cited as defining that interpolation. Values outside the table are rejected instead of extrapolated. CCR oxygen metabolism and diluent/loop consumables are not inferred from OC RMV in the normal CCR ledger.

The Tools redesign adds interaction policy around these functions, not a new decompression model. Best Mix and END use versioned PPO2/narcotic-policy identifiers and require the actual oxygen and helium fractions. With oxygen and nitrogen treated as narcotic, `END = ((Pambient × (1 − FHe)) − Psurface) × metersPerBar`; with nitrogen alone narcotic, `END = ((Pambient × FN2 ÷ 0.79) − Psurface) × metersPerBar`. Best Mix solves those equations in reverse while respecting the PPO2 ceiling and returns the complete active-constraint set.

SAC / RMV accepts either direct measured surface-gas volume or cylinder water volume plus a gauge-pressure drop, preserving the selected mode and source values. A moving-depth sample uses the arithmetic mean of start/end absolute pressure and therefore assumes depth changes linearly with time. Emergency Gas (Rock Bottom / Minimum Gas and Simplified Bailout) evaluates only an explicitly entered schedule through the same gas-integration primitive used by planner ledgers. OC requires an explicit team size and can return schedule gas without a cylinder or add one-cylinder pressure/sufficiency output; CCR requires one bailout cylinder. Every cylinder check requires an explicit reserve, including an intentional zero. It does not generate a decompression schedule or a CCR bailout plan; CCR bailout continues to use the exact-trigger planner path. Ambient pressure is absolute, cylinder pressure is gauge, and gas-use calculations consume gauge-pressure deltas.
